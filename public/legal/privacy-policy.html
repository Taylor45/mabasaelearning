2. When this policy does not apply

When I design or build learning content for a client, such as courses, assessments or learning platform set-ups, I usually act as an operator for that client. The client is then responsible for its learners’ personal information, the client’s own privacy policy applies, and my written agreement with the client governs how I handle it. Section 3 explains how I protect project information in either case.

This policy also does not cover my employment or any work I do for an employer. Those are governed by that organisation’s own policies.

3. Protecting your project

Whether I act as an operator or as the responsible party, I treat everything you share for a project as confidential. This is how I keep it that way.

What I treat as confidential Examples
Your content Briefs, source material, subject matter expert input, existing courses, templates and other intellectual property
Learner and staff information Names, contact details, results, completion records and any other personal information of your learners or staff
Access details Learning management system logins, admin accounts, links to staging or live systems, keys and passwords
Business information Pricing, strategy, unreleased products, internal documents and anything you mark as confidential

  1. Use only for your project. I use your information only to deliver the work you hired me for, and I do not disclose it except to the service providers I need to deliver it (section 8) or where the law requires.

  2. Written terms. My proposal or agreement with you includes confidentiality terms. Where I handle learner or staff personal information for you, it also records that you are the responsible party and that I process it only on your instructions.

  3. Only what is needed. I ask you not to send me real learner or staff personal information unless the project needs it. Where I can, I work with anonymised or sample data and test accounts instead.

  4. Limited access. I ask for the lowest level of access I need, use only accounts you give me for the project, and ask you to remove my access when the project ends.

  5. AI tools. I do not put your confidential content or personal information into AI tools unless you have agreed in writing, and I choose tools with appropriate confidentiality terms.

  6. Return or deletion. When the project ends, I return or delete your project files if you ask, except for copies I must keep under the law or our agreement, such as invoices.

  7. Problems. If a security compromise affects your project information, I tell you without undue delay so you can act, and I report it to the Information Regulator where the law requires.

4. Featuring a project in my portfolio

My Website shows examples of my work, so I may want to feature a project once it is finished. I do it only with your consent.

  1. No consent, no feature. Nothing from a client project, including the client’s name, logo, screens, storyboards, video or results, appears on the Website without your prior written consent.

  2. Separate and specific. I ask for consent separately from the project agreement. The request says what I want to show, whether I would name you or anonymise the work, and for how long.

  3. You review first. You can see the planned content before it goes live, and I make the changes you reasonably ask for.

  4. Confidential material stays out. Even with your consent, I never publish learner or staff personal information, access details or anything you have told me to keep confidential. I replace it with sample content or remove it.

  5. Testimonials. If I want to use a quote, your name or your logo as a testimonial, I ask for your written consent for that too.

  6. Withdrawing consent. You can withdraw your consent at any time by emailing me. I then remove the project from the Website within 10 working days. I cannot recall copies that others have already saved or shared.

  7. Without consent. I may describe my skills in general terms, for example “a higher education course for a client”, without identifying you or your project.

  8. Employer work. I show work I did as an employee only with that employer’s permission.

11. Security

I apply appropriate and reasonable technical and organisational safeguards to protect personal information, as section 19 of POPIA requires. If a security compromise affects your information, I will report it to the Information Regulator and to you, as section 22 of POPIA requires.

12. Your rights and complaints

Under POPIA you may:

  1. ask whether I hold your personal information, and ask for access to it;

  2. ask me to correct or delete information that is inaccurate, outdated, excessive or unlawfully obtained;

  3. object, on reasonable grounds, to processing based on legitimate interests;

  4. object to direct marketing, or withdraw your consent, at any time; and

  5. complain to the Information Regulator.
